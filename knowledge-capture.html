What can you send us?

Send existing instructions first. In week one, each person describes their role and walks us through a typical shift, on a call or in a recording. We use that outline to guide the detailed walkthrough.

Voice notes ◉

Start with a spoken overview of your shift. Then, as you do a real task, describe what you notice, what you decide and when you make an exception. Set aside time if you can. Detailed recording can continue for a week or longer per role.

Short videos ▷

Demonstrate a setup, handoff or inspection. Tell us which role does it and what a good result looks like.

Photos with context ▧

Add clear captions or refer to the photo in the accompanying recording so we can connect it to the right step.

Documents & interviews ▤

We read your checklists and manuals before comparing them with recordings. Staff can answer follow-ups between shifts. We can put discovery questions into a small app with an assistant that asks for more detail, or arrange a call.

What if a recording shows someone’s personal details? ▤

We actively check walkthroughs for identifying details. If a customer’s name appears on a dispatch screen, we blur or blank it in the image, mute it in the audio and remove it from the transcript wherever it appears. If the instruction needs to say who does something, we use a role such as “shift lead.”

We check after upload. The transcription provider receives the original recording. When we detect identifying details, we redact them and immediately delete the identifiable copy Sonaway received. We can also agree to stop processing, delete the file and request a new recording when specified types of identifying information are detected.

You can request deletion of the redacted recording too. We generally recommend keeping it initially so a reviewer can trace a questionable instruction back to the recording and correct it at its source.

Only the redacted version is kept in the project library. You choose who can open that recording and who can see the finished guide. Giving someone access, including Sonaway, does not give them a copy with the detected details intact.

Our systems support aggregate reporting. We do not build tools to track people by identifying details captured on shift. Optional named sign-in accounts are separate from the operational material. If you take over the system, your team is responsible for its ongoing privacy and security.

How many people need to participate?

You can designate one person who knows the job and has authority to define it. We usually suggest two experienced people per role, often one per shift, and generally no more than about three for capture. Most can record while they work. Key contributors and approvers may need a few sessions of one to two hours each. More people are welcome at the testing stage to try the drafts and flag errors.

When will we see a draft, and how quickly do you follow up?

You can typically expect something useful to review, such as a preliminary role or shift outline, one to two weeks after the agreed project start date. We set that date after discovery. Completion depends on the complexity of the role, when recordings arrive and how quickly reviewers answer open questions.

For a typical complete batch, we aim to send follow-up questions within two business days: Monday morning to Wednesday, or Friday morning to Tuesday in a normal workweek. Several roles submitting full-shift recordings together can take longer. We compare new material against existing instructions first so we only ask questions that help settle a gap or disagreement.

From a shift outline to an approved checklist

You first review a role description and a high-level outline of the shift. Once that looks right, we work through individual tasks with you. Here’s how a packer’s explanation of a label mismatch becomes a checklist.

  1. You record the task

    Talk into your phone as you pack a box. “I check the label against the order. If they don’t match, I set the box aside.” Send the recording and any checklist you use today.

  2. We check what’s missing

    We listen, write down the steps and compare them with your checklist. One question is still open, so we ask the shift lead: “Who checks the replacement label before this box ships?”

  3. You check the draft

    The lead replies, “I print the new label and check it against the order.” We add that detail to the draft. You can request changes or approve the checklist between shifts. We publish it after your approval.

What if the checklist and the shift leads disagree?

We send you a breakdown of the disputed step. For example, the written checklist assigns order release to the supervisor, both leads say the packer does it, and the question to settle is who is authorized to release the order.

During setup we agree with you who decides each kind of question. You can assign decisions by role, set a hierarchy or require several stages of approval. Your team can approve the agreed sections while the disputed step stays open. We record the decision in that step once the designated approver or approvers resolve it.

Can we record in Spanish and approve the guide in English?

Yes. Your team can record and send documents in its working language. You choose which version is authoritative. For example, you can approve the English procedure and retain the Spanish source. We use multiple translation checks. Bilingual spot checks depend on the language and package.

A custom search tool can retrieve an approved instruction in one language and answer in another. Staff can use familiar equipment names. If a nickname could refer to two machines, the assistant should ask which one they mean. When you maintain your own platform, you control how its search and updates work.

Who updates the guide when the job changes?

For a one-time delivery, your team handles future edits in your chosen platform. With a custom build, we can give you a guided way to submit a change, such as a short video of a new printer check, and route it for approval. You can use that workflow even if you maintain the system yourselves. With a maintenance agreement, changes can be routed to Sonaway for follow-through. Human approval is the default; the exact workflow is agreed for your project.

How do we test whether a one-workflow pilot is complete?

We agree on the job and what someone new should be able to do using just the guide or by asking questions of the documents. For dispatch handover, that might mean preparing the handover, checking it and passing it to the right person.

Before approval, invite managers, shift leads and other experienced staff to ask questions they already know the answers to, including exceptions. They can use a temporary web chat connected to the draft documents and flag inaccurate or missing answers. We use those flags to correct the material. The testing chat is included in the pilot at no extra build cost.

What do we keep if we stop after the pilot?

You keep the documents, SOPs and checklists we delivered. If the pilot scope includes a transferable knowledge base and chat, we can hand that system over too. The temporary chat used to test drafts is included for testing; continued hosting or a system handover depends on the scope. If you expand into a larger project, the approved work carries forward and the amount you paid for the pilot can count toward the larger project.
